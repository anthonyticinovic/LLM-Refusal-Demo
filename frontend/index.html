A direction in activation space — the geometry, before the language model

Two classes of points. One direction r̂, estimated from the data, not drawn by hand. Two operations on it: remove the component along r̂, or add some amount of it. Watch which one destroys the difference between the classes and which one merely relocates it — that asymmetry is the whole finding.

This is a toy, and it should be read as one. These are 130 synthetic points per class in 3 dimensions. A real language model's residual stream has thousands — 2048 for the model driving Layer 2, and 4096+ for the models in the original paper. Two offset Gaussian blobs separate along their own difference-of-means by construction; that part proves nothing. What carries over is the arithmetic: the projection h·r̂, the subtraction h − (h·r̂)r̂, and the addition h + αr̂ performed here are the identical operations Layer 2 applies to real activations from a trained model. The claim that these operations mean something lives in Layer 2, not on this page.

r̂ is estimated from a random half of the points and every number above is measured on the other half. That mirrors Layer 2, where the direction is extracted from one prompt set and judged on prompts it never saw. It is also why ‖Δcentroid‖ does not reach exactly zero under full ablation: the leftover is estimation error, in directions r̂ does not cover.

What to notice

Ablation destroys the signal. Push it to 100% and the gap goes to zero. Not "small" — zero, by construction: subtracting (h·r̂)r̂ leaves nothing along r̂ to measure. The two classes become indistinguishable on this axis.

It only looks like the cloud collapses to a line. It collapses to the 2D plane perpendicular to r̂, and you are seeing that plane edge-on — necessarily so, because the camera angle that shows r̂ at full length is the same one that puts your eye in the plane. Drag to rotate and it opens back up. Three dimensions became two; one direction's worth of information was removed, not all of it.

Injection moves the baseline, and nothing else. Sweep α across its full range: both means slide together, and the gap between them does not move at all. You are not making one class more like the other — you are relocating both.

That asymmetry is the point. In Layer 2, ablating the refusal direction removes refusal broadly, including on prompts the model would normally refuse — the signal is gone. Injecting it induces refusal on prompts that are plainly benign — the baseline moved far enough that everything reads as refusal. One operation deletes a feature; the other forces it.

Resample, and watch r̂ move. The direction is recomputed from freshly drawn points every time, via difference-of-means. It is an estimate with sampling error, not a constant baked into this page.
